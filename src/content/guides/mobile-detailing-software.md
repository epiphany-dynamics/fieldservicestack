**Judge mobile detailing software on one thing: whether it keeps a per-vehicle record. Test every tool against a rained-out booking, a package priced by vehicle size, travel and setup buffers, an add-on sold at the curb, before and after photos, and a maintenance reminder tied to the last service date.**

Most booking tools sold to detailers were built for salons and studios. They handle a person, a time slot, and a service name. That model breaks the first time a customer books an express interior for "the SUV" and your tech shows up to a third-row Suburban with a dog crate in the back, priced as a mid-size.

## Start with the vehicle, not the calendar

**A detailing customer is not an address. It is a household with two or three vehicles, each on a different package, condition, and cadence. If the software attaches history to the person only, your tech arrives blind and your pricing drifts. The vehicle needs its own record with its own service log.**

Ask the vendor to show you a customer with three vehicles on file. Then check what each vehicle record can hold:

- Year, make, model, and your own size class (compact, sedan, mid SUV, full-size, three-row, truck)
- Plate or last six of the VIN, plus color, so the tech confirms the right car before touching it
- Coating or sealant applied, the date, and the maintenance wash interval it requires
- Standing condition notes: pet hair, smoker, sap exposure, kids' car seats, ceramic-coated wheels
- Access facts: driveway or street, garage clearance, outdoor spigot, exterior outlet, gate code, HOA rules on water runoff

That last group is what separates a profitable route from a wasted drive. A tech who learns on arrival that the building bans hose water in the garage has already burned the slot. ## Weather and travel buffers set your real booking window

**Detailing is one of the few field trades where the sky cancels the day. Your software has to reschedule an entire morning in a few taps, not one job at a time, and it has to hold travel plus setup time as real occupied capacity rather than padding you add by hand.**

Two capabilities matter here, and both are easy to test in a demo.

Bulk reschedule. Ask to move every job on a specific date to the same slot the following week, with one customer notification per job. If the answer involves opening each visit individually, price that in: a six-job day is six edits, six texts, and at least one booking you forget to move.

Travel and setup as blocked time. An illustrative express package might run 90 minutes of labor, but with a 35 minute drive, 10 minutes to stage water and cords, and 10 minutes to break down, the slot consumes closer to two and a half hours. Software that books back-to-back on labor time alone will overbook every tech you hire. Look for per-job drive time pulled from the previous stop, plus a fixed setup and teardown buffer by service type.

## Price the package to the vehicle, not the menu

**Detailing pricing has three layers: base package, size class multiplier, and condition surcharge. A tool that only stores flat-price services forces techs to negotiate at the curb or eat the difference. You want size-aware pricing plus on-site add-ons the customer can approve from their phone.**

The failure case is specific. A customer books a full interior online at the sedan rate. The tech opens the door to heavy pet hair and two years of spilled juice. That job is now 90 extra minutes. If the tech cannot add a documented surcharge, get a signature or a tap-to-approve, and push the revised total to the invoice, one of three bad things happens: the tech rushes the work, the shop absorbs the labor, or the customer gets an unexplained higher bill and disputes the charge.

What to require:

1. Service catalog with per-size-class pricing, not one price per service
2. Condition surcharges as selectable line items with preset amounts
3. Add-ons a tech can attach on site (engine bay, headlight restoration, ozone treatment, clay and sealant, ceramic maintenance top-up)
4. Customer approval captured in the record before the extra work starts
5. Invoice and payment collected at the vehicle, with the card on file charged after approval

## Photos are your callback defense

**Before and after photos are not marketing assets first. They are your record of pre-existing damage. Rock chips, curb rash, cracked trim, and failing clear coat all get blamed on the detailer eventually. Photos must attach to the vehicle record with a timestamp, not float in a text thread.**

Build a required photo set the tech cannot skip: four corners, wheels, existing paint defects, interior front, interior rear, cargo area. Then the same set after. The callback scenario is predictable. Three days later a customer texts about a scratch on the rear quarter panel. With timestamped arrival photos on that vehicle's record, you resolve it in one message. Without them, you either eat a paint correction or lose a repeat account.

Check that photos survive the customer view too. Sending an after-set with the invoice reduces disputes and does more for referrals than any social post.

## Score your shortlist before you buy

**Run every candidate through the same eight checks in a live demo with your own data. Score each as pass or fail, not one to five. A tool that fails size-based pricing or vehicle-level history is not a cheaper option, it is a different product that will cost you at the curb.**

| Check | What to ask in the demo | Pass looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle record | Add three vehicles to one customer | Each has its own service history and notes |
| Size-based pricing | Price the same package for a sedan and a three-row SUV | Different totals from one catalog entry |
| Curb add-ons | Add an $85 pet hair surcharge mid-job | Customer approves, invoice updates |
| Travel buffer | Book two jobs 30 minutes apart geographically | Calendar blocks drive time automatically |
| Bulk reschedule | Move a full rain day to next week | One action, notifications sent |
| Photo capture | Complete a required before and after set | Photos attach to the vehicle, timestamped |
| Rebooking cadence | Set a 90 day maintenance reminder | Reminder fires with vehicle context |
| Payment at vehicle | Charge card on file after approval | Funds captured before the tech leaves |

## When a detailing-specific tool is the wrong buy

**If you run one van solo at roughly a dozen jobs a week, a niche detailing app can cost more in migration and accounting gaps than it returns. Small detail-first tools often ship thin accounting sync, weak multi-tech dispatch, and no real reporting once you add a second truck.**

The honest sequence: prove the vehicle record matters with your current tool plus a disciplined notes field. If you are losing money to mispriced size classes, missed rebookings, or damage disputes, buy for that. Switching platforms mid-season also costs you the history you just spent a year building, so make the move in your slow month, export everything first, and confirm the new system accepts vehicle-level import rather than customer-level only.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need detailing-specific software or will general field service software work?

General field service software works if it supports asset-level records, size-based pricing, and photo capture tied to that asset. Many platforms built for HVAC and plumbing already do, because equipment records and vehicle records solve the same problem. Test those three capabilities before assuming you need a niche tool.

### How should the software handle rain cancellations?

You want bulk rescheduling of an entire day, automatic customer notification, and a way to mark the reason so you can see how many slots weather costs you per month. A tool that requires per-job edits will quietly train you to skip notifications, which produces no-shows on the makeup date.

### What is the right way to price by vehicle size?

Define three to five size classes and set a multiplier or fixed price per class inside one catalog entry, so online booking and tech estimates use the same numbers. Keep condition surcharges separate from size so you can see which one is actually driving your margin variance.

### Can the software drive repeat maintenance bookings?

Yes, if reminders trigger from the last service date on the vehicle rather than a generic customer campaign. A coated vehicle on a 90 day maintenance wash and a family SUV on a twice-yearly interior should generate different reminders at different intervals from the same system.

### What should a tech see before arriving at the job?

Vehicle year, make, model, color, and plate, the exact package and add-ons sold, condition notes from prior visits, water and power access, parking details, and photos from the last service. Anything missing from that list becomes a phone call from the driveway.
