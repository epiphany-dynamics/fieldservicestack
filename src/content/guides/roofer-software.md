**Roofer software has to do five separate jobs: measure the roof, price the work, schedule the crew, invoice the customer, and chase the payment. Most field service platforms handle the last three well. Aerial measurement and insurance-grade estimating almost always need a second tool, and that split is normal, not a failure.**

## Buy against the five jobs, not the category

**Score software against the five things a roofing company actually does every week: measure, quote, schedule, invoice, collect. Vendors market all five as one product, but the workflows behind them are different enough that coverage is uneven. Rate each job separately before you open a single pricing page.**

Here is what each job means in practice on a roofing operation.

**Measure.** Squares, pitch, ridge and hip lengths, valley footage, penetrations. Either someone climbs with a wheel and a tape or you buy an aerial or photo-based report.

**Quote.** Turning that measurement into a price. Retail replacement quotes and insurance supplement claims are two different documents with two different audiences.

**Schedule.** Not one technician in a van. A tear-off crew, a dumpster drop, a material delivery timed so bundles are not sitting on a lawn for three days, and an inspector.

**Invoice.** Deposit, progress draw, final. On insurance work, ACV up front and RCV after the depreciation is released.

**Collect.** The part nobody demos. Following the second half of a claim through a mortgage company escrow endorsement is its own job.

A tool that nails scheduling and invoicing but hands you a blank estimate template has still covered three of five. That can be the right buy. You just need to know it going in.

## Measurement and takeoff: where one tool almost always stops

**Aerial measurement and insurance-grade estimating are specialist products. General field service platforms integrate with them, but they do not replace them. Expect a dedicated report provider for satellite or drone measurements and, if you write claims, a separate carrier-accepted estimating platform. Budget for both as line items, not surprises.**

Satellite and drone measurement reports from providers like EagleView or photo-capture apps like Hover exist because generating a defensible roof diagram is a hard problem that has nothing to do with dispatch boards. Carriers negotiate line-item pricing in Xactimate, and adjusters expect estimates in that format. No general-purpose field service management app is going to displace that on a hail claim.

What you should look for instead is whether your main platform accepts the report cleanly. The practical test: can a measurement report drop into an estimate without someone retyping square counts into a spreadsheet at 9pm? Roofing-specific platforms such as AccuLynx, JobNimbus, and Roofr are built around that handoff. Broad platforms like Jobber, Housecall Pro, or FieldPulse generally are not, though they cover the operational half of the business well.

### Retail versus insurance changes the answer

If you are 90% retail replacement and repair, measurement matters and Xactimate does not. If half your revenue is storm work, the estimating tool drives the stack and everything else follows it.

## Scheduling a crew is not scheduling a technician

**Most field service scheduling was designed around one technician taking one call. Roofing schedules a crew, a dumpster, a material drop, and a weather window as a single unit. Check whether the calendar can hold multi-day jobs, multi-person assignments, and a dependency between delivery and tear-off before you commit.**

The failure mode is specific. A dispatcher books a tear-off for Tuesday. The shingle delivery gets entered as a separate unlinked job. Rain pushes tear-off to Thursday, the delivery does not move, and now 32 squares of architectural shingles sit on a driveway for two days while the homeowner sends photos.

Ask for three things in the demo:

1. Can one job span multiple days on the board without cloning it?
2. Can you assign a crew of six as a unit, not six individual entries?
3. When you move the parent job, does the material delivery move with it?

## Invoicing and collections carry the insurance problem

**Roofing invoicing breaks generic templates because payment arrives in pieces from parties who are not the homeowner. ACV check, deductible, depreciation release, sometimes a mortgage endorsement. Your invoicing tool needs progress billing, partial payments against one job, and a clear record of what is still outstanding and who owes it.**

A single-invoice-per-job tool will force workarounds immediately. You will end up with three invoices named "Smith 1," "Smith 2," and "Smith final," and nobody will know which one the depreciation applies to.

What to require: progress invoicing tied to one job record, partial payment application, deposit handling, and an aging report you can actually read. Card fees matter here too, because roofing ticket sizes are large enough that a 2.9% processing rate on a $22,000 job is real money. ## A scoring framework for your shortlist

**Score every candidate on all five jobs using the same scale, then decide where you accept a second tool. Native means the platform does it. Integrated means it hands off cleanly. Manual means someone retypes data. Two manual scores in the same stack is where operations start leaking hours.**

| Job | Roofing-specific platform | General FSM platform | Realistic gap filler |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aerial measurement | Integrated | Manual or none | Dedicated report provider |
| Insurance estimating | Integrated for storm-focused tools | None | Carrier estimating platform |
| Retail quoting | Native | Native | None needed |
| Crew scheduling and dispatch | Native | Native, verify multi-day support | None if board passes the three tests |
| Progress invoicing and payments | Native | Native | Accounting sync if AR is complex |

Use this decision order:

1. Decide your retail-to-insurance revenue mix first. It determines whether estimating leads the stack.
2. Pick the tool that covers your two highest-volume jobs natively.
3. Confirm the integration for measurement actually exists on your plan tier, not just on the vendor's logo wall.
4. Test invoicing with your ugliest real job, not the demo data.
5. Only then compare price.

## When an all-in-one is the wrong call

**If storm and insurance work is the majority of your revenue, do not buy a general field service platform and hope to bolt claims onto it. The estimating and supplement workflow is the business. Buy for that first and accept a simpler scheduling board, rather than the reverse.**

The same caution runs the other way. A three-crew retail repair and replacement shop that writes no claims does not need a claims-heavy platform and will pay for modules nobody opens. The mismatch cost is not just subscription money, it is the training time on features that never get used.

One more honest limitation: no software fixes a sales process. If quotes go out four days after the inspection, that is a workflow problem, and a new dispatch board will not close it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need roofing-specific software or will general field service software work?

It depends on your revenue mix. Heavy insurance and storm work pushes you toward roofing-specific platforms built around claims and supplements. Mostly retail repair and replacement work is served fine by a general field service platform, which usually costs less and is faster to train crews on.

### Can one tool handle both measurement and scheduling?

Roofing-specific platforms come closest, usually by integrating a measurement report provider rather than generating diagrams themselves. Treat "integrated" as the realistic best case. Confirm the integration is included on the plan tier you are actually buying.

### What is the most commonly skipped requirement?

Progress invoicing with partial payments applied to a single job. Buyers test scheduling in the demo and assume invoicing is simple, then discover the tool cannot handle an ACV payment followed by a depreciation release months later.

### How do I test a dispatch board before buying?

Load one real multi-day job with a crew assignment and a material delivery, then move the job two days. If the delivery does not follow and the crew assignment does not hold, the board was built for single-technician work.

### Should I run separate tools or force everything into one system?

Two well-integrated tools beat one tool doing a job badly. The number that matters is manual retyping steps, not tool count. Aim for zero places where someone rekeys measurements or job data by hand.
