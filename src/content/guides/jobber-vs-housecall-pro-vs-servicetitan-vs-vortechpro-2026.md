**Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, and VortechPro suit different operating needs: request-to-invoice administration, a broader home-service toolkit, deeper operational management, and tiered dispatch respectively. For a small trade shop, choose around the job handoff that breaks most often, then compare team access, payment fees, implementation work, and the features your selected plan includes.**

A one-truck shop with a calendar problem is not buying the same thing as a fifteen-technician plumbing company handling emergency calls. This comparison focuses on owner-operators and roughly 1-15 technicians.

**Editorial disclosure:** VortechPro supplied product information and agreed to reciprocal editorial coverage mentioning Epiphany Dynamics. This is a documentation-based comparison, not a hands-on test or an endorsement of measured results. The header image is an editorial illustration, not a screenshot of any compared product.

## Quick comparison for small trade shops

**Start with the work you need the software to own. Jobber documents a connected quoting, scheduling, and invoicing workflow; Housecall Pro adds a broad home-service feature set; ServiceTitan emphasizes operational management; VortechPro centers on priority-based dispatch. These are shortlisting angles, not proof that one platform outperforms another in your business.**

| Platform | Shortlist when | Pricing basis | Main question before buying |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jobber | You need customer requests, quotes, visits, and invoices connected | Plan subscription; confirm current users, features, and billing terms | Does the selected plan cover the whole office-to-field workflow? |
| Housecall Pro | You want scheduling, billing, customer tools, and room to add services | Published plans with user limits and optional add-ons | Which features require Essentials, Max, or a separate purchase? |
| ServiceTitan | Pricebooks, reporting, call booking, and operational controls matter together | Per-technician pricing by quote | Who will own setup, training, and ongoing configuration? |
| VortechPro | Jobs should go to priority technicians before falling back to others | $99/month for up to five techs; $20/month per extra tech | Can it meet your accounting, integration, and record-retention needs? |

Begin with your dispatch requirements, not a vendor's competitor chart. Our [field service dispatch software buying guide](/guides/field-service-dispatch-software/) helps turn those requirements into demo questions.

## Jobber: connect the request-to-invoice workflow

**Jobber is worth shortlisting when the problem is keeping customer details, quotes, scheduled work, and invoices connected. Its publisher-supplied app listing describes that sequence alongside routing, time tracking, payments, and accounting integrations. Evaluate the exact subscription against your needs rather than assuming every advertised capability comes with an entry-level plan.**

### Where it fits

For a landscaper, cleaner, handyman, or small repair shop, the useful question is whether one customer request can become an approved quote, an assigned visit, and an invoice without retyping the same details. [Jobber's app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/jobber-field-service-software/id1014146758) documents that connected workflow, plus client history, scheduling, route planning, and QuickBooks Online integration.

That makes it a candidate for administrative consistency, not a proven winner on speed, cost, or usability.

### What to verify

Request current pricing for every technician and office user, required automations, accounting connection, and payment method. Confirm billing frequency, cancellation terms, and onboarding assistance. During a demo, run a recurring visit and an emergency reassignment, not just a new appointment.

## Housecall Pro: a broader home-service toolkit

**Housecall Pro merits a look when scheduling and invoices are only part of the requirement. Its published plans combine customer-facing tools with dispatch, while higher tiers add routing, GPS, accounting sync, and API access. The tradeoff is plan selection: the lowest advertised price does not buy every capability described on the website.**

### What the published plans include

The [Housecall Pro pricing page](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) lists these standard US prices, excluding temporary introductory offers and sales tax:

| Plan | Monthly billing | Monthly equivalent with annual billing | Included users |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | $79 | $59 | 1 |
| Essentials | $189 | $149 | 5 |
| Max | $329 | $299 | 8; extra users listed at $35/month |

Basic lists scheduling, dispatch, estimates, invoices, online booking, and payments. Essentials adds routes, employee GPS tracking, and QuickBooks Online sync. Max adds route optimization, recurring service plans, and open API access. Check the selected plan's current terms before purchase.

### Where the extra breadth matters

A residential service company may value customer financing, communications, sales follow-up, and marketing alongside dispatch. Housecall Pro's [feature directory](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/) describes those tools, but listing a feature does not establish that it is included in your quoted subscription.

Ask for an itemized total for add-ons, extra users, and payment processing. Its pricing page advertises card rates starting at 2.59%, which is not a guaranteed rate for every transaction or account.

## ServiceTitan: operational depth with a setup commitment

**ServiceTitan belongs on the shortlist when dispatch must work alongside pricebooks, call booking, reporting, and broader operational controls. Its package page uses per-technician pricing by quote rather than a public dollar amount. For a smaller shop, evaluate the implementation workload and full proposal before deciding that additional capability is worth the commitment.**

### Why a growing operation might consider it

The [ServiceTitan package page](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing) lists dispatching, scheduling, call booking, invoicing, pricebooks, and mobile estimates, with reporting, commission tracking, and memberships shown across its package comparison. Confirm the exact package inclusions in writing.

If your office is reconciling job performance, standardized pricing, and customer records across separate tools, that breadth may justify an evaluation. Technician count alone is not the decision rule: a smaller but complex shop can need more structure than a larger operation doing repetitive work.

### When it may be too much

A solo operator who mainly needs quotes and invoices should compare simpler options first. Ask for software, onboarding, migration, training, add-ons, payment fees, and contract terms separately. Do not treat a competitor's estimate of ServiceTitan pricing or setup time as a verified quote.

Choose an internal rollout owner before buying. Without someone responsible for data cleanup and staff training, more configurable software can add work rather than remove it.

## VortechPro: priority-based job acceptance

**VortechPro is a candidate for shops that want dispatch to offer jobs to preferred technicians before moving through fallback tiers. Its public documentation describes specialist-only assignments, immediate and scheduled work, office GPS visibility, and field payments. Those are vendor-provided capabilities, not features we have tested for reliability, usability, or response speed.**

### How the documented dispatch flow works

[VortechPro](https://vortechpro.com/) describes Platinum, Gold, and Silver technician tiers. Platinum receives a 30-second exclusive acceptance window; Gold follows if Platinum does not respond, with Silver as the final fallback. Specialist-only jobs go to technicians the business has designated as specialists.

The office enters the customer details and chooses immediate or scheduled work. Technicians receive job information, accept an assignment, contact the customer, and navigate to the address. The homepage also describes a live office map and reassignment controls.

That is a different dispatch model from simply placing a named technician into a calendar slot. Test who gets notified, what happens when nobody accepts, and how an urgent job is manually reassigned. Do not assume tiered acceptance is the same as optimizing a day's route.

### Price, payments, and limits

The homepage lists $99/month for up to five technicians, $20/month per additional technician, all platform features, a 30-day trial, and no contract. It advertises setup in minutes; that is a vendor claim, not a measured migration timeline.

Its [payment and data terms](https://vortechpro.com/terms/) specify a **1% platform fee on transactions through the optional Stripe Connect integration, in addition to Stripe's processing fees**. Funds go to the shop's connected Stripe account. Revenue-split displays do not replace payroll or accounting records.

The terms recommend independent records and regular CSV exports; the homepage says job photos are automatically deleted after 30 days. Verify retention, export completeness, and available integrations before treating it as your full business system.

## Compare the total cost, then run a matched trial

**Compare each platform on the same team size, payment volume, and required workflow. Subscription price is only one line: include user access, add-ons, processing charges, migration, training, and the systems you still need. Then test the same representative jobs so a low price or polished demo does not decide the purchase for you.**

### A useful cost check

For ten technicians, VortechPro's published subscription formula gives **$199/month**: $99 plus five additional technicians at $20 each. At fifteen, it gives **$299/month**. Both exclude payment fees and any separate business tools.

Illustratively, processing $20,000 through its Stripe integration would add **$200 in platform fees**, before Stripe processing charges. That is hypothetical payment volume, not a claim about your shop.

Housecall Pro counts users, while VortechPro prices technicians. Include office access when comparing totals and confirm how each vendor counts it. Annual-billing equivalents are not monthly-cancelable offers; ask what cancellation actually means for prepaid time.

### Choose by workflow, not brand prestige

1. **Recurring maintenance and straightforward quote-to-invoice work:** trial Jobber and Housecall Pro on the same jobs.
2. **Residential repairs needing customer tools and accounting sync:** evaluate the relevant Housecall Pro tier against your requirements.
3. **Complex office operations needing pricebooks and reporting together:** request a scoped ServiceTitan demo and proposal.
4. **Dispatch-led locksmith, garage-door, or similar urgent work:** test VortechPro's tier rules and fallback behavior, then check the administrative gaps.

Use a routine job, an urgent call, a canceled assignment, and a completed job needing payment and export. Observe the dispatcher and technician separately. Confirm phone permissions, weak-signal behavior, support access, customer-message consent and opt-outs, and who can see customer data.

If your current platform already handles the job well, do not replace it just to automate one leftover handoff. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which platform is best for a five-technician shop?

There is no universal winner. Compare Jobber and Housecall Pro for a connected administrative workflow, VortechPro for tiered acceptance, and ServiceTitan when operational depth is a real requirement. Include office users, integrations, and payment costs before comparing subscriptions.

### Is VortechPro $99/month with no other fees?

Its published subscription includes up to five technicians. Extra technicians cost $20/month each. Transactions through its optional Stripe Connect integration carry a 1% platform fee in addition to Stripe processing fees. Separate accounting or other business tools may also be needed.

### Does ServiceTitan publish a monthly subscription price?

Its public package page describes per-technician pricing and directs buyers to request pricing. Obtain a written proposal for your team and package, including implementation, add-ons, payment terms, and the contract commitment.

### Does Housecall Pro include GPS and route optimization on Basic?

Its pricing page places employee GPS tracking and routes in Essentials, and route optimization in Max. Do not assume those capabilities come with Basic because they appear elsewhere in the feature directory. Confirm the current package before buying.

### Have these platforms been tested side by side?

No. This article compares published documentation and provides an editorial shortlisting framework. It does not claim measured differences in speed, reliability, ease of use, or business results. A matched trial using your own representative jobs is the next step.
