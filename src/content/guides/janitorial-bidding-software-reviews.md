**Janitorial bidding software should capture measurements, workload assumptions, wage and consumables costs, and the approved proposal before a contract is signed. This guide gives a pre-approval checklist, shows where bidding differs from recurring invoicing, and explains how to judge any tool without a star rating or a ranking.**

A commercial cleaning bid is a set of promises made before anyone has cleaned the building. When the customer calls in week three to say the restrooms were supposed to be serviced daily, your supervisor needs to see what was measured, what was assumed and what the customer approved. Most "janitorial bidding software reviews" skip that question and list features. This page starts from the approval and works backward.

## What a bid record must hold before approval

**Capture five things: measured areas with floor types, a task-based workload by frequency, a weighted wage, consumables cost, and the exact proposal that was approved. If any of those lives only in the estimator's head or a spreadsheet nobody saved, the first callback about scope cannot be settled with records.**

Use this table as a pre-approval gate. It is an editorial checklist, not a feature score.

| Record | Capture before approval | Question to ask at sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| Measurements | Dimensions or total size, floor type, description per area | Does this match the walkthrough? |
| Workload | Hours by task, area, frequency and floor type | Who built these hours, and from what? |
| Wage | Each rate and each employee's hours, rolled into a weighted average | Is this the crew we will actually staff? |
| Consumables | Estimated usage, estimated cost, chosen profit | Is paper and poly included in the price or billed separately? |
| Proposal | The branded document, attachments and approver | Is this the version the customer saw? |

## Measurements and floor types

**Store each area with dimensions or total size, floor type and a short description, and keep the version that matches the walkthrough. Occupancy and customer requirements change during bidding, so the software should let you edit measurements while keeping a record of what the approved proposal assumed.**

Say you walk a 12,000 square foot office. The lobby is tile, the open floor is carpet, and the break room is vinyl. If the software holds one lump total, the crew lead cannot tell later why vacuuming hours were set where they were. Per-area entries with floor type give the scheduler something real to build a night route from.

CleanGuru's CleanBid feature page describes this kind of entry: measurements by actual dimensions or total size, with floor type and description, editable if occupancy or requirements change. That supports the criterion. It does not show the measurements are accurate. A measuring error still becomes a pricing error.

## Workloading, wages and consumables

**Ask whether hours come from tasks, area, frequency and floor type, not one square-feet-per-hour guess. Then check that wages reflect each employee's rate and hours, and that consumables carry an estimated usage, cost and profit line. All three are assumptions, so approval means someone reviewed them.**

### Workload by task and frequency

Daily restroom service and monthly floor burnishing do not share a rate. A tool that builds hours from task, area, frequency and floor type gives you a number you can argue with line by line. A single blended rate hides which assumption is wrong. Vendor pages that advertise this approach describe a feature. They do not give you a production rate, and you should not borrow one from marketing copy.

### Weighted wage

Illustrative numbers only: two cleaners at $16 an hour working 20 hours each per week, and a lead at $19 working 10 hours. The weighted average is (16 x 20 + 16 x 20 + 19 x 10) / 50, which is $16.60. Quoting the lead's $19 across the whole job overstates labor. Quoting $16 understates it. The check before approval is whether that staffing mix is the one dispatch can actually schedule.

### Consumables

Many customers want paper towels, toilet paper and liners included in the monthly price. The bid should hold an estimated usage based on building occupancy, an estimated cost and the profit you chose to add. Treat it as a bid assumption. Actual use in a busy building can run differently, so note who owns the overage.

## Proposal approval and handoff to billing

**The approved proposal should be the editable, branded document that went to the customer, with cover letter, insurance certificates and attachments, plus a note of who approved the price. Recurring invoicing starts after signature and is a different job from bidding.**

Keep two stages apart. Pre-contract work answers "what did we measure, assume and promise?" Post-contract work answers "what did we deliver and bill this month?" A bidding tool that also invoices is a convenience. It does not remove the need to freeze the approved bid first.

### A four-step approval check

1. Re-open the measurements and confirm they match the walkthrough notes.
2. Read the hours by task and ask who set each assumption.
3. Confirm the staffing mix behind the weighted wage.
4. Save the exact proposal PDF and attachments, with the approver's name and date.

## Limits of vendor feature pages

**Vendor feature pages describe modules, not results. They support criteria for your evaluation but do not prove accurate measurements, real production rates, savings or margin, and they cannot rank products. Test any tool on a past bid you already know the outcome of before trusting it with a new one.**

This article did not test any product hands-on, and it gives no star rating or best-product pick. The CleanGuru descriptions of its measurement, workloading, consumables, wage and proposal modules are vendor claims. They are useful as a list of things to demand from any tool, including ones we have not looked at.

A practical test: take a closed bid from last year, rebuild it in the candidate tool, and compare the hours and price to what the crew actually needed. If you cannot reconstruct the old bid, you will not trust the next one either.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should janitorial bidding software capture before a proposal is approved?

Measured areas with floor types, hours built from tasks and frequency, a weighted wage, a consumables estimate and the exact proposal sent. Together these let you explain the price later without relying on memory.

### Is bidding software the same as invoicing software?

No. Bidding happens before the contract and records assumptions. Invoicing happens after and records delivered service. Some products cover both, but approve the bid first and carry the agreed price into billing.

### Can I use a vendor's square feet per hour figure for my bid?

Use your own crew's history. A vendor feature page describes how a calculator works, not what your crew will produce in your buildings. Treat any imported rate as an assumption to check.

### How do I review a janitorial bidding tool fairly?

Score it against the checklist above using a closed bid you know well. Record where it needed manual workarounds. Ignore claims that cannot be tested with your own data.
