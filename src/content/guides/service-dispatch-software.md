**Service dispatch software assigns jobs to technicians, tracks the day in real time, and pushes job details to a phone in the field. For most field service businesses the practical shortlist is Jobber, Housecall Pro, FieldPulse, Workiz, Service Fusion, and ServiceTitan. The right pick depends on crew size, average job length, and how often your schedule breaks.**

## What service dispatch software actually does on a busy day

**Dispatch software is the layer between the phone ringing and a technician knocking on a door. It holds the job, the customer history, the assigned tech, the drive window, and the parts list in one record. Everything else you care about, estimating, invoicing, and reporting, hangs off that single record.**

Picture a Tuesday with four trucks and 22 stops on the board. At 9:40 a homeowner is not home, so a 90 minute slot opens in the north zone. At 10:15 a commercial account calls back on a job you closed Friday, and the warranty visit has to land today. At 2:00 a tech finds a failed control board and needs an estimate approved before he leaves the driveway.

Good dispatch software makes all three of those moves take under a minute. You drag the callback into the open slot, the assigned tech gets a push notification with the original job notes attached, and the estimate goes out from the phone with photos on it. Weak software makes each move a phone call, a text, and a note somebody forgets to write down. That difference is the entire buying decision, and it is why our [field service dispatch software guide](/guides/field-service-dispatch-software/) treats reschedule speed as the primary test rather than feature count.

## Best service dispatch software compared

**No single platform wins for every trade. Route based work with short jobs rewards a fast calendar and mobile invoicing. On demand emergency work rewards call handling and same day assignment. Larger residential shops with a real call center reward capacity planning and reporting depth. Match the tool to how your work actually arrives.**

| Software | Best fit | Dispatch strength | Trade off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jobber | 2 to 15 techs doing route based or scheduled work | Clean drag and drop calendar, tight quote to invoice flow | Cost rises as you add users and higher tier features |
| Housecall Pro | Residential trades with heavy inbound consumer booking | Online booking, automatic customer notifications, card payments | Job costing depth is lighter than enterprise tools |
| FieldPulse | Growing crews that want depth without enterprise pricing | Scheduling plus price book, custom forms, and estimating | More setup work before it pays off |
| Workiz | On demand trades such as locksmith, garage door, appliance repair | Call tracking tied to job records, fast same day assignment | Less natural for long multi day projects |
| Service Fusion | Shops with several office and dispatch users | Dispatch grid with live technician status | Interface feels dated to some crews |
| ServiceTitan | 15 or more techs in mature HVAC, plumbing, or electrical | Capacity planning, dispatch boards, deep reporting | Highest cost and the longest implementation |

## A five step way to shortlist without wasting a month

**Most owners demo six platforms, get impressed by six demos, and end up choosing on price. Reverse that. Define your two hardest dispatch scenarios first, then make every vendor perform them live in the demo. A tool that stumbles on your worst Tuesday will not improve after you pay for it.**

1. **Count your true schedule changes.** Track one normal week: no shows, callbacks, add on emergencies, and parts delays. That number, not your tech count, tells you how much dispatch power you need.
2. **Write your two worst scenarios.** For example, a same day warranty callback that must be assigned to the original tech, and a two hour cancellation that has to be backfilled from a waitlist.
3. **Demo those exact scenarios.** Ask the rep to do it on screen while you watch the clock. Count clicks.
4. **Check the technician side, not the office side.** Have an actual tech open the mobile app on a job with 14 photos and a signed estimate. If it is slow in the parking lot, it is slow in a crawlspace.
5. **Price the whole stack.** Add users, payment processing, GPS, marketing add ons, and onboarding. Compare total annual cost, not headline monthly cost.

If two finalists are close after step five, read a head to head before you sign. Our [FieldPulse vs Housecall Pro comparison](/compare/fieldpulse-vs-housecall-pro-2026/) is a useful example of how two tools that look nearly identical on a feature grid diverge on estimating and job costing.

## Dispatch features that quietly decide the winner

**Feature lists all look the same. The details that determine whether dispatchers keep using the software are unglamorous: how a callback links to its parent job, whether a tech can see yesterday's notes offline, and whether a rescheduled visit notifies the customer automatically instead of requiring a phone call.**

Use this checklist during demos:

- Drag and drop rescheduling that updates the technician and the customer in one action
- Callback and warranty visits that link to the original job with its notes, photos, and parts
- Offline mode on the mobile app, tested in a basement or a metal building
- Estimating from the truck with photo attachments and customer approval on the spot
- Invoicing and payment collection before the tech leaves the property
- Recurring job templates for maintenance agreements and seasonal contracts
- Technician status the dispatcher can see without texting: en route, on site, completed
- Job costing that captures labor hours and parts against the job automatically

Missed calls belong on this list too, because an unanswered call never becomes a dispatch record at all. If your team is in a crawlspace when the phone rings, pair whatever software you choose with a [missed call text back service](/guides/missed-call-text-back-service/) so the lead lands in the same queue as everything else.

## When new dispatch software will not fix your problem

**If your schedule falls apart because estimates sit unapproved for four days, techs never write parts on the ticket, or nobody owns the dispatch board, software will expose that faster than it fixes it. New tools amplify whatever process already exists. A shop with no process gets an expensive, well organized version of chaos.**

Two honest exceptions to the buy now instinct. First, a shop of one or two people doing scheduled work with steady repeat customers can often run on a calendar and an invoicing tool for another year without losing money. Second, a shop in the middle of a busy season should not migrate. Data migration, price book setup, and retraining during peak weeks costs more in blown jobs than the software saves. Migrate in your slow quarter.

Before you shop, run a simple audit of where jobs stall between the call and the paid invoice. The [dispatch to invoice audit walkthrough](/guides/how-to-automate-business-processes/) gives you the sequence, and the result usually narrows your shortlist by itself.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between dispatch software and scheduling software?

Scheduling software books a time. Dispatch software manages the day after it starts: reassigning techs, handling emergencies, tracking status, and rerouting when a job runs long. Most modern field service platforms do both, but lightweight scheduling tools often lack the live board and technician status view that real dispatching requires.

### How much does service dispatch software cost?

It ranges from entry level plans aimed at solo operators to enterprise contracts with implementation fees in the thousands. Cost drivers are user count, payment processing rates, and add on modules. Always price the full annual total including onboarding, since a cheap monthly plan with a large setup fee can cost more in year one.

### Can dispatch software handle same day emergency callbacks?

Yes, and this is the single feature worth testing hardest in a demo. Ask the vendor to create a callback linked to an existing closed job, assign it to the original technician, and notify the customer. Watch whether the original job notes, photos, and parts carry over automatically or have to be retyped.

### Do I really need GPS tracking on technicians?

It helps with accurate arrival windows and drive time analysis, and it settles disputes about time on site. It is not required for most small crews, and some technicians resent it. If you add it, explain the reason as arrival accuracy for customers rather than surveillance, and make the arrival window benefit visible to the team.

### What size business actually needs ServiceTitan?

Generally 15 or more technicians in residential HVAC, plumbing, or electrical, with a dedicated dispatcher and someone who will use the reporting. Below that, the implementation effort and cost usually outrun the benefit, and a mid tier platform delivers most of the daily dispatch value.
