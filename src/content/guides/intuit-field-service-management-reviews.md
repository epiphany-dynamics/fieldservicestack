## What the legacy pages say about editions

**The saved Corrigo pages name three edition-related items: QuickBooks Enterprise Platinum for truck inventory, QuickBooks Enterprise Advanced Inventory for bins, and QuickBooks Desktop Pro, Premier and Enterprise for the Time Card module. The inventory page also says all versions update inventory levels. None of it is a current requirements list.**

The vendor's overview page describes a dispatcher or service manager who can see which technicians are on site and reassign jobs. It also describes invoicing, payments and other information syncing with QuickBooks, and contract terms, service intervals and customer discounts managed from a dashboard. Those are advertised capabilities. The page does not say which QuickBooks edition each one needs.

That gap matters in a real shop. A dispatcher moving a callback from one technician to another cares about the schedule. The bookkeeper cares about whether the resulting invoice lands in the right QuickBooks company file. The overview page does not connect those two concerns to an edition, so you cannot assume it.

## Inventory: all versions, Platinum, and bins

**Corrigo's inventory page says every QuickBooks version tracks stock and that the sync updates levels regardless of version. It names Enterprise Platinum for deducting from each technician's truck as well as the warehouse. A separate page says IFSM deducts from locations, not from bins.**

The wording is easy to over-read, so here are the three separate claims.

### Levels update in any version

The vendor says that when an approved IFSM invoice syncs into QuickBooks, the sync updates inventory levels inside QuickBooks, regardless of version. That sentence is about inventory levels. It is not a statement that every edition supports every IFSM feature.

### Truck deduction names Platinum

The same page says that with QuickBooks Enterprise Platinum, inventory is also deducted from the appropriate truck. Platinum is legacy naming. Ask the vendor what the equivalent is today, and whether it applies to your edition.

### Locations, not bins

The Advanced Inventory page says QuickBooks Enterprise lets you set up bins inside multiple inventory locations. When technicians add items to an IFSM invoice, stock comes off the technician's inventory location during sync. The vendor says IFSM does not support deducting directly from bins.

An illustrative example, not vendor data: a plumbing shop stocks water heater elements on a van, in a labeled bin. Under the vendor's description, the technician's invoice reduces the van's location count, and nobody should expect the bin count to move. If your warehouse manager audits by bin, plan for that difference. ## Questions to put to the vendor

**Turn each historical mention into a written question before you rely on it. Ask which QuickBooks edition and version you run, which modules your workflow needs, and whether each is still sold for that edition today. Get the answer in writing, because old pages do not confirm current availability.**

Use this checklist in order:

1. Write down your QuickBooks product (Desktop or Online), edition and version.
2. List the workflows you need: truck stock, bin-level counts, field time cards, recurring maintenance contracts.
3. Ask which of those the vendor supports on your exact edition today, not under legacy names.
4. Ask what each optional module costs and whether it is still sold.
5. Ask how invoice sync handles a job where a technician adds parts after the customer signs.
6. Run the answers against your invoicing process. Our guide to [field service invoicing software](/guides/field-service-invoicing-software/) covers where the cash gap opens between a finished job and a paid invoice.

Step 5 is a question, not a finding. The saved pages do not describe that case.
