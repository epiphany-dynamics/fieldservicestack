**A CRM for roofers earns its keep in the two weeks after a hailstorm, not in a quiet March. Judge it on lead intake speed, duplicate address handling, adjuster and claim follow-up tracking, and how cleanly an approved scope becomes a scheduled crew day. Generic contact management fails all four.**

Roofing sales does not behave like HVAC maintenance or plumbing service calls. Volume arrives in bursts, half your pipeline is controlled by someone who does not work for you, and a lead can sit dormant for eleven weeks before a depreciation check makes it a job. That shape is what your CRM has to survive.

## Why roofing breaks a generic CRM

**Most CRMs assume a steady trickle of leads and one contact per deal. Roofing runs the opposite way: months of low volume, then several hundred inbound leads in a week, with three parties on every file. The data model has to hold a property, an owner, a claim, and a crew day together.**

On an insurance job the record is really about a roof, not a person. Ownership can change mid claim. The bill goes to a mortgage company. The decision maker for scope is an adjuster you will speak to once, by phone, on their schedule. A contact-first CRM makes you fake all of this with custom fields and free-text notes, and free text is where follow-up goes to die.

Retail replacements and repairs behave more normally, which is why some roofers run fine on a standard field service platform until their first big storm. If you are still deciding whether one platform can carry sales, production, and billing at once, [Roofer Software: What One Tool Can Honestly Cover](/guides/roofer-software/) works through that scope question. This guide stays narrowly on the lead-to-job pipeline.

## The storm-season stress test

**Run every demo against a hail week, not a normal week. The questions that matter: how fast does an inbound lead become a bookable inspection, does the system catch the same address entered by two canvassers, and can a manager see stalled files without opening records one at a time.**

### Intake speed

During a storm surge, calls arrive faster than an office can answer them. Every unanswered call is a lead a competitor gets, because homeowners in a damaged neighborhood call three companies in one afternoon. Your CRM needs to accept leads from a web form, a call, a text, and a canvasser's phone, and it needs to timestamp them so you can measure speed to first contact. A [missed call text back service for field service pros](/guides/missed-call-text-back-service/) is the cheapest patch here, and it only works if the reply lands in the same pipeline your reps already watch.

### Deduplication and territory

Two reps working adjacent streets will enter the same address. So will the homeowner filling out your form after the door knock. Without address-level deduplication you get commission disputes and two inspections booked on one roof. Ask the vendor to show you what happens when a duplicate address is entered, live, in the demo. Many will show you a warning banner. Fewer will show you a merge that keeps both reps' notes and preserves the original lead source.

### Stalled-file visibility

The dangerous lead is not the one that says no. It is the one where the adjuster meeting happened and nothing has been entered for nineteen days. You want a view that sorts by days in current stage, not a dashboard of totals.

## Adjuster follow-up belongs in the pipeline, not the notes

**Insurance work has its own stage sequence that sits between "sold" and "scheduled." If those stages live in a notes field, nobody can report on them, nobody gets reminded, and supplements quietly go unsubmitted. Each stage needs a date, an owner, and an automatic next action.**

At minimum, model these as real pipeline stages: inspection completed, claim filed, adjuster meeting scheduled, adjuster meeting held, scope sheet received, supplement submitted, supplement approved, ACV check received, work scheduled, work completed, final invoice and depreciation released.

The two stages that leak money are supplement submitted and depreciation released. Both depend on a carrier acting, both have no natural deadline, and both are invisible unless your CRM ages them. A rule as simple as "flag any file sitting in supplement submitted for more than ten business days" turns a passive queue into a call list. That is also why storage matters: the scope PDF, the adjuster's contact, the photo report, and the supplement request should attach to the file, not live in one rep's email.

## What to check before you buy

**Score candidates on pipeline mechanics rather than feature counts. The table below maps each capability to why it matters on a roofing file and how to test it in a demo instead of taking the sales engineer's word for it.**

| Capability | Why it matters on a roofing file | How to test it in the demo |
|---|---|---|
| Custom pipeline stages | Insurance stages differ from retail stages | Ask them to build a supplement stage on screen |
| Address-level dedupe | Two canvassers, one roof | Enter the same address twice, live |
| Stage aging and alerts | Supplements stall silently | Show a list sorted by days in stage |
| Photo and document attachment | Scope sheets, claim numbers, inspection photos | Upload a multi-page PDF to a lead |
| Multi-contact records | Owner, adjuster, mortgage company | Add three contacts with different roles |
| Lead source tracking | Storm spend versus retail spend | Pull a report by source and close rate |
| Estimate to job handoff | Approved scope becomes a crew day | Convert an estimate without retyping scope |
| Mobile entry offline | Canvassers work where signal is poor | Ask what happens with no service |

Use a simple scoring pass: rate each row 0 to 2, and treat anything under 12 as a platform you will be working around within a season. That threshold is an illustrative rule of thumb, not a benchmark, but it forces a decision instead of a feature comparison spreadsheet nobody rereads.

## From approved scope to a crew on the roof

**The handoff is where roofing CRMs most often fail quietly. An approved scope carries squares, pitch, layers, ventilation, and material color. If any of that gets retyped by hand into a scheduling tool, the crew eventually shows up with the wrong shingle on the wrong day.**

Two things prevent it. First, the approved scope should convert into a job with line items intact, so material orders and crew hours come from the same numbers the carrier approved. Second, scheduling has to respect crew capacity and material delivery, not just an open calendar slot. Billing closes the loop. Insurance jobs invoice in pieces: ACV, supplement, deductible, then recoverable depreciation. ## When a roofing CRM is the wrong purchase

**If you run mostly retail replacements and repairs, do fewer than roughly fifteen jobs a month, and never touch supplements, a roofing-specific CRM adds cost and setup time for stages you will never use. A general field service platform with good scheduling and invoicing will serve you better.**

The same applies to a crew-of-two repair operation. The complexity that makes a roofing CRM valuable is claim complexity, and if you do not carry claims, you are paying for it anyway. Revisit the decision the first season you file more than a handful of supplements, or the first time you cannot answer how many files are waiting on depreciation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a roofing CRM and general field service software?

A roofing CRM models the claim: adjuster contacts, scope documents, supplement stages, and split billing. General field service software models the visit: schedule, dispatch, invoice, done. Companies doing heavy insurance work need the first. Retail-only roofers usually do fine with the second.

### Do I need a separate tool for canvassing and door knocking?

Not always, but check how leads enter. Some CRMs include territory maps and mobile lead capture. If yours does not, the canvassing app you choose has to push leads into the CRM with the rep and address attached, otherwise dedupe and commission tracking break immediately.

### How should I track supplements in a CRM?

As dedicated pipeline stages with dates, not notes. Supplement submitted and supplement approved should both be stages that age, so a file sitting in submitted for two weeks appears on a follow-up list automatically instead of waiting for someone to remember it.

### Can one CRM handle both retail and insurance roofing?

Yes, if it supports separate pipelines. Retail moves inspection to estimate to sold to scheduled. Insurance adds six or more carrier-dependent stages. Forcing both through one stage list produces reporting that describes neither accurately.

### What should I migrate when switching roofing CRMs?

Move open files, their documents, and the last twelve months of closed jobs for warranty lookup. Older records can stay in an export. Migrating everything is the most common reason a switch stalls midway and both systems end up half used.
