**Junk removal software should be judged on one chain: the curbside quote, the load photos, the truck's capacity, the disposal facility, and the ticketed dump fee that lands back on the invoice. If a tool tracks appointments but loses the weight ticket and the before photo, it will not survive a billing dispute.**

## What a junk removal job record has to hold

**A junk job record is not a standard service call. It needs load fraction, material classes, access conditions, crew size, truck assigned, disposal destination, ticket number, and timestamped photos at both ends. Every one of those fields either sets the price, sets the cost, or defends the invoice weeks later.**

A generic work order gives you customer, address, arrival window, and a notes box. That is not enough for this trade. Price the same 3/8 load two ways and the gap shows immediately: a garage cleanout of cardboard and old shelving goes to the transfer station at one mixed rate, while the same volume containing two mattresses, a refrigerator, and four tires picks up per-item fees at three separate facilities.

The fields that actually move money:

- **Load fraction:** minimum charge, 1/8, 1/4, 3/8, on up to full, each with its own price break.
- **Material class per item:** general, mattress, appliance with refrigerant, e-waste, tires, construction debris, yard waste, and anything you decline.
- **Access:** curbside, garage, basement stairs, elevator, long carry.
- **Crew and truck:** two people on the 15 yard dump body, three on the 20 yard trailer.
- **Destination and ticket:** facility name, scale ticket number, weight, fee paid.
- **Photos:** before, loaded truck, site cleared, and the disposal receipt.

If the platform cannot hold those last two lines against the job, the chain breaks at exactly the point where disputes happen.

## Pricing a load you cannot see until you arrive

**Phone quotes in junk removal are ranges, not prices. The software has to carry a quoted range from the call, let the crew confirm real volume and material surcharges on site, capture a signature on the revised number, and push that signed amount into the invoice without anyone retyping it.**

The on-site re-quote is the business. It is also where most software fails quietly, because the office quote and the field total live in two different places and get reconciled by memory at the end of the day.

Here is an illustrative walk-through using made-up rates, since your local disposal costs will differ: the call taker quotes "1/4 to 3/8 load, $260 to $340." The crew arrives, sizes the pile at 3/8, and finds one mattress at a $35 disposal surcharge, a refrigerant appliance at $45, and a basement carry worth a $40 labor add. Signed on site at $460. If the tablet cannot show the customer that itemized jump from $340 to $460 before anything goes in the truck, you are having a chargeback conversation in three weeks.

What to require: quote ranges rather than single numbers, on-site adjustments with reason codes attached, e-signature on the revised total, and card capture at the curb. ## Chain of custody from curb to scale house

**Chain of custody means the invoice can be rebuilt from artifacts rather than memory. Six pieces do it: the signed on-site estimate, before photos, a loaded truck photo, the disposal ticket showing weight and fee, the cleared site photo, and a paid invoice that references all five.**

Run this as a literal checklist on every job:

1. Signed estimate with itemized surcharges, timestamped at the address.
2. Before photos of the pile from two angles, geotagged if the app supports it.
3. Loaded truck photo taken before leaving the property.
4. Scale ticket or facility receipt attached to the job, not the truck's glovebox.
5. Cleared site photo, same two angles as the before shots.
6. Invoice that displays or links the ticket number and photo set.

Why it matters beyond tidiness: property managers ask for proof of removal before releasing payment, estate and hoarding jobs generate "something of mine was in that load" calls, and commercial accounts increasingly want a diversion figure showing what went to donation, scrap, or recycling rather than landfill. None of that is answerable without the ticket trail. ## Dispatch built around truck capacity, not open time slots

**A junk crew's real constraint is cubic yards remaining, not hours remaining. A calendar that only shows open blocks will book a full-load job onto a truck already 70 percent full, and the crew burns most of an afternoon on an unplanned dump run that nobody scheduled.**

Four things separate capacity-aware dispatch from a color-coded calendar:

- Remaining capacity per truck is visible on the board, updated as jobs are completed.
- Dump runs are scheduled events with facility hours attached, because transfer stations close early on Saturdays and a missed cutoff means a loaded truck sits overnight.
- Route order accounts for load size, putting the big pull before the facility closes rather than at 4:30.
- Same-day add-ons are sized against remaining capacity before the office promises a window.

This is the same class of problem that equipment-constrained trades face, where the asset rather than the labor hour caps the day. ## Scoring a shortlist before you sit through demos

**Score candidates on the six capabilities that carry junk-specific risk, weight them by what actually breaks your week, then make each vendor perform them on a live job record during the demo. Ask them to attach a scale ticket and reprint the invoice with it, on the call, not in a follow-up email.**

| Capability | Why it matters | Weight | Demo test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Load-fraction pricing | Volume, not hours, is your price unit | 5 | Build a 3/8 load quote from a template |
| On-site re-quote with signature | Protects the upcharge from dispute | 5 | Change $340 to $460 and capture a signature |
| Per-item material surcharges | Mattress, freon, tire, e-waste fees | 4 | Add three surcharge items to one job |
| Disposal ticket capture | Cost side of job costing | 4 | Attach a ticket and see it on the invoice |
| Photo set tied to the job | Callback and claim defense | 4 | Pull before and after photos six months back |
| Truck capacity on the board | Prevents unplanned dump runs | 3 | Show remaining yards for two trucks today |

Score each 1 to 5, multiply by weight, and total. Anything that scores below 60 percent of the possible points on the first four rows will leak margin no matter how good the calendar looks. ## When junk-specific software is overkill

**One truck, one transfer station, flat volume pricing, and no per-item surcharges means a general field service tool plus a disciplined photo habit will cover you. Junk-specific capability starts paying once you run two or more trucks, bill commercial accounts, or owe someone a weight or diversion report.**

Two honest limits are worth stating. First, no field service platform talks directly to your transfer station's scale software. Ticket entry stays manual in every tool available today, so if a vendor claims automatic dump fee import, ask precisely which facilities and get the list in writing. Second, photo retention has a real cost. A crew shooting eight photos per job across three trucks generates serious storage volume in a year, and some plans cap it or charge per gigabyte. Ask about the retention window before you build a compliance process on top of it.

Switching mid-season is its own risk. If you are heading into a spring cleanout rush, run the new system in parallel on one truck for two weeks rather than cutting over the whole fleet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use general field service software for junk removal?

Yes, if your pricing is simple and you only use one disposal facility. The friction appears with per-item surcharges and dump fee tracking, since most generic tools treat those as line items with no link to the facility ticket or the material class that generated them.

### How should software handle a phone quote that changes on site?

It should carry the quoted range into the field, let the crew adjust volume and add surcharges with a reason code, display the revised itemized total on a tablet, and capture a signature before loading. Any system that requires calling the office to change a number will lose upcharges.

### What photos should the crew capture on every job?

Before shots from two angles, the loaded truck before departure, the cleared site from the same two before angles, and the disposal receipt. That set answers nearly every callback, including missing-item claims and property manager proof-of-removal requests, without anyone relying on recollection.

### How do I bill dump fees without eating the margin?

Attach the scale ticket to the job so the actual fee posts against that job's cost, not a monthly bucket. Then compare ticketed cost to quoted price per job. Operators who only reconcile disposal at month end cannot tell which load types are losing money.

### Does junk removal software handle donation and recycling routing?

Some tools let you record multiple destinations per load with separate tickets, which is what diversion reporting requires. Others assume one stop per job. Test this specifically if you split loads between a donation center, a scrap yard, and the landfill on the same run.
