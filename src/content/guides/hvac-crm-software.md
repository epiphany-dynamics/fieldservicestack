**An HVAC CRM stores equipment, not only contacts. The useful record ties install date, model and serial, warranty expiration, refrigerant type, filter sizes, and last service date to a specific address. Generic CRMs track the person and lose the equipment, which is exactly what drives renewals, accurate estimates, and fewer callbacks.**

## Why the HVAC customer record is an equipment record

**In HVAC, the thing you service is the equipment, not the household. A homeowner moves and the condenser stays. A property manager changes and the rooftop units keep aging. Build the record around the system at the address, and history, warranty status, and renewal timing all stay attached where technicians need them.**

A contact-first CRM answers "who is this person and what did we sell them." An HVAC shop needs to answer something narrower: what is installed at 412 Rockwell Drive, when was it put in, who installed it, what has failed before, and what parts fit it. Those answers do not live in a deal stage.

### One address can hold several systems

A two-story house with a zoned system, a detached shop with a mini split, and a small commercial customer with four rooftop units all break the one customer equals one record assumption. If your CRM cannot nest multiple pieces of equipment under one service location, technicians end up guessing which unit last week's note referred to. ## Where a generic CRM breaks on a real HVAC job

**Generic CRMs model deals and contacts, so HVAC detail lands in free-text notes. That works until dispatch needs filter sizes before a tune-up, an estimator needs the install date to check warranty, or a callback needs last week's readings. Notes do not sort, filter, or trigger anything, so the data quietly stops working.**

Three places it shows up in a normal week:

- **Dispatch.** A tune-up gets assigned without anyone knowing the unit takes two 20x25x5 media filters, so the truck arrives empty and the visit becomes a return trip. Sorting the board by job type is only half the problem; see how to keep maintenance, service, and install work from colliding in [HVAC Dispatch Software: Handling Three Job Types at Once](/guides/hvac-dispatch-software/).
- **Estimating.** A compressor fails on a four-year-old system. If the install date and manufacturer warranty registration are not on the record, the estimator quotes full parts and either loses the job or eats the margin when the homeowner produces their paperwork.
- **Callbacks.** A tech returns to a no-cool complaint with no access to last visit's superheat and subcool readings, so diagnosis restarts from zero on your dime.

## The maintenance agreement renewal math your CRM should drive

**Maintenance agreements are the most predictable revenue in a service business, and renewal rate is the lever. An equipment-aware CRM knows which agreements expire next month, which visits were actually delivered, and which systems are old enough to justify a replacement conversation. That turns renewals into a scheduled workflow instead of an annual scramble.**

## The equipment fields worth capturing on every visit

**Capture equipment data at the job, not from the office. Every visit should update the same record: model and serial, install date, warranty dates, refrigerant type, filter sizes and quantity, static pressure or key readings, and photos of the data plate. Anything a technician cannot enter in under a minute will not get entered.**

| Field | Why it earns its place |
| --- | --- |
| Model and serial | Drives correct parts lookup and manufacturer warranty checks |
| Install date and installer | Decides warranty coverage and whether you own the workmanship |
| Parts and labor warranty end dates | Prevents quoting covered parts as billable |
| Refrigerant type and charge | R-410A versus R-22 versus R-454B changes repair versus replace math |
| Filter sizes and quantity | Loaded on the truck before a tune-up, not discovered on site |
| Last service date and findings | Makes a callback a continuation instead of a restart |
| System age and condition score | Feeds the replacement conversation list |
| Data plate photo | Backstops every field above when handwriting fails |

Make the equipment tab the first screen a tech sees on arrival, not a buried subform. Adoption follows convenience.

## How to score HVAC CRM software before you switch

**Score HVAC CRM candidates on data structure first and features second. Ask whether equipment is a first-class object, whether agreements auto-generate visits, whether the mobile app shows history offline, and whether you can export everything. Demos sell scheduling screens; the data model decides whether the system still helps you in year three.**

1. **Equipment as an object.** Can you attach multiple units to one location, each with its own service history and warranty dates? If the answer is a custom field, keep looking.
2. **Agreements that generate work.** The system should create the spring and fall visits automatically and flag ones never delivered.
3. **Warranty logic.** Install date plus warranty terms should surface on the estimate screen, not require a lookup in a manufacturer portal.
4. **Technician capture speed.** Time an actual tech entering a full equipment record. Over ninety seconds and it will be skipped in August.
5. **Renewal reporting.** You need expiring agreements by month, renewal rate by campaign, and equipment past a chosen age threshold.
6. **Export and ownership.** Confirm you can pull equipment records, not just contacts, in a usable format.

## When an HVAC-shaped CRM is the wrong fix

**An equipment-shaped CRM will not fix a shop that never collects the data. If technicians skip the equipment tab, migration will import blank fields and you will pay more for the same guesswork. New construction and install-heavy companies with little service work also get less from it than a maintenance-driven residential shop.**

There is a sequencing point here too. If your real leak is unanswered phones during a heat wave, equipment records will not recover that revenue. Fix intake first, then structure the data. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an HVAC CRM different from field service management software?

In practice most HVAC shops buy one system that does both. The distinction that matters is whether the customer record carries equipment and agreement data or only contact and deal data. A CRM bolted onto scheduling without an equipment object leaves you tracking systems in spreadsheets.

### Can I use a general CRM with custom fields instead?

You can, and small shops do. The limits appear when you need to filter by warranty expiration, auto-generate agreement visits, or show history offline on a phone in a crawlspace. Custom fields store data; they rarely trigger workflows.

### What should I migrate first when switching systems?

Active maintenance agreements and their equipment records. Those drive scheduled revenue, so a gap there costs money immediately. Historical invoices and closed jobs can follow later or stay archived in the old system.

### How do I get technicians to actually enter equipment data?

Make it fast and make it required at one moment, usually before the invoice can be closed. Data plate photo capture with model and serial entry beats long forms. Review completeness weekly for the first month, then monthly.

### What renewal rate should we expect from maintenance agreements?

There is no honest universal benchmark, and any single number you see quoted deserves scrutiny. Measure your own baseline for a full year before setting a target, then track whether a specific change to reminders or call lists moves it.
