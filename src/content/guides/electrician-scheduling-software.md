**Electrician scheduling software is dispatch, estimating, and invoicing software built around permit-bound, inspection-bound electrical work. The right pick handles multi-day rough-in jobs, service calls, and callbacks on one board, keeps van inventory and labor rates attached to the work order, and bills the same day the panel is energized.**

## Why electrical scheduling breaks generic field service tools

**Electrical work mixes two job shapes that fight each other on one board: short service calls and multi-day construction phases gated by inspections. Generic tools assume every job is a one-visit appointment, so rough-in, trim, and final inspection get typed in as separate unlinked jobs and the job history disappears.**

A service van doing panel swaps and troubleshooting needs tight same-day routing. A crew on a tenant improvement needs a schedule that spans three weeks, holds four electricians, and pauses when the inspector moves the date. Most platforms are strong at one shape and tolerable at the other. Decide which one carries most of your revenue before you sit through a single demo.

### The callback problem

An electrical callback carries warranty and liability weight that a mowing callback does not. When a kitchen circuit trips two weeks after a remodel, the dispatcher needs the original job, the tech who pulled the wire, the panel schedule photo, and the material list on one screen. Tools that attach photos to a visit instead of to the property turn that lookup into a phone call.

### Permits and inspections

Permit numbers and inspection windows are scheduling constraints, not notes. If the software cannot block a trim visit until the rough-in inspection passes, your dispatcher is holding that rule in their head, and it fails the week they take vacation.

## The capability checklist that separates real candidates

**Seven capabilities decide whether a platform survives contact with electrical work. Score each one during the demo, not from the feature page. Ask the rep to perform the test in the right column live, in their own sandbox, while you watch. Anything answered with "that is on the roadmap" scores zero.**

| Capability | Why it matters for electrical | How to test it in the demo |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-visit job linking | Rough-in, trim, and final belong to one job record | Build a three-phase job and produce one invoice from it |
| Property-level history | Callbacks need the prior panel photo and wire list | Search a past address and count clicks to the panel photo |
| License and skill routing | Only some techs can take a service change or fire alarm work | Assign a restricted job to an apprentice and see if it warns |
| Assembly-based estimating | A 200A service change is one assembly, not 40 line items | Build a service change quote from saved assemblies |
| Van and truck inventory | Breakers, wire, and fittings must post to the work order | Consume 250 feet of wire on a job and check the cost |
| Progress and same-day billing | Construction needs draws, service needs card on site | Issue a 40 percent progress invoice on the open job |
| Offline capture | Basements, mechanical rooms, and new builds have no signal | Put the phone in airplane mode and complete a work order |

## A five step framework for scoring vendors

**Score vendors in a fixed order so the demo does not lead you. Start from your own job mix, weight the capabilities that mix demands, then test, then price. Buying in the reverse order is how contractors end up with a cheap platform that cannot hold a three-visit job.**

1. **Split last year's jobs by type.** Count service calls, small projects, and multi-week construction. Whichever bucket holds the most revenue sets your weighting.
2. **Weight the seven capabilities.** A service-heavy shop weights routing, dispatch board speed, and same-day billing. A construction-heavy shop weights job linking, progress billing, and labor cost tracking.
3. **Run the live tests.** Use the table above. Record the click count for each test, since click count predicts whether your dispatcher will actually use the feature at 7 a.m.
4. **Check the accounting handoff.** Have the rep sync one invoice to your accounting file and inspect how it lands: item mapping, sales tax, and job costing.
5. **Price it last.** Only after two or three vendors have passed the tests. A good dispatch board is a strong reason to overrule a per-seat price difference.

## What electrician scheduling software actually costs

**Seat price is rarely the largest cost. Implementation, data migration, payment processing, and training hours usually add more in year one than the subscription does. Model all five before you compare vendors, and use your own labor rate rather than the vendor's savings estimate.**

## When new scheduling software is the wrong fix

**If your schedule falls apart because calls go unanswered, because estimates sit for a week, or because two techs quit, new software will document the problem instead of solving it. Scheduling tools organize demand you already captured. They do not create capacity and they do not answer a ringing phone.**

Two failure patterns come up often. First, missed calls: a platform with a perfect dispatch board still loses the job if nobody picks up at 4:50 p.m. Fix intake first, then the board. Second, quote lag: if your close rate is slipping because estimates take four days to send, the constraint is your estimating process, not your calendar.

Also be honest about shop size. A two-truck operation with a shared calendar and a solid intake process can run clean for another year. The switch cost is real, and mid-season migrations tend to land badly.

- Choose one crew and one job type, usually residential service.
- Import 90 days of customer history, not your entire archive.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need electrician-specific software or will general field service software work?

General platforms work well for service-heavy shops that mostly run one-visit calls. They strain on multi-week construction with inspection gates and progress billing. Run the seven capability tests above. If a general tool passes the job-linking and progress-billing tests, trade-specific branding adds little.

### How do I schedule apprentices and journeymen without overbooking a license?

Look for skill or license tags on both the technician record and the job type, plus a warning when the assignment breaks the rule. A soft warning is usually better than a hard block, because dispatchers need to override during emergencies. Verify the override leaves an audit trail.

### What is the fastest way to cut callbacks with scheduling software?

Force a completion checklist with required photos before a tech can close the work order. Torque checks, panel schedule photo, and a labeled circuit list catch most repeat visits. The second lever is property-level history, so the next tech sees what was done rather than guessing.

### Should a two-truck shop buy this now or stay on a shared calendar?

Stay on the calendar if you answer every call, send estimates within a day, and invoice on site. Move when you start losing job history, missing follow-ups, or spending more than an hour a day rebuilding the schedule. The trigger is administrative load, not truck count.

### How long does a switch usually take?

Plan for a 30 day pilot on one crew, then a staged rollout over the following month. The long pole is almost always data migration and accounting mapping, not training. Import a limited history window first so you are not cleaning ten years of duplicate customer records during your busy season.
