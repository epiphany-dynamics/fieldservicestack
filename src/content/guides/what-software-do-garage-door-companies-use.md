**Garage door companies typically run a field service platform such as ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, or Workiz, paired with QuickBooks for accounting. The right choice depends on whether the software can handle same-day emergency dispatch, track torsion springs and opener parts on each truck, capture before-and-after photos, and invoice on site. This comparison tests each option against that record.**

## The short answer: what garage door companies actually run

**Most garage door companies land on one of four platforms. ServiceTitan serves larger operations with pricebook and truck stock depth. Housecall Pro and Jobber cover one-to-ten-truck shops with scheduling, quoting, and payments. Workiz fits dispatch-heavy shops built around phone-booked emergency calls. Smaller crews sometimes start on Google Calendar plus QuickBooks and outgrow it fast.**

The category matters less than the workflow. A garage door business is a parts-on-the-truck business with an emergency call problem. Broken torsion springs, dead openers, and off-track doors come in as urgent phone calls, the tech arrives with a stocked truck, swaps the part, photographs the door, and collects payment before leaving the driveway. Software that cannot support that chain in one mobile pass creates callbacks and unbilled parts.

Here is how each platform profile fits:

- **ServiceTitan**: deep pricebook, job costing, and inventory by truck. Best for shops over five trucks with an office dispatcher.
- **Housecall Pro**: strong mobile app, online booking, and card-on-file payments. Fits residential service shops.
- **Jobber**: clean scheduling and quoting, client hub for approvals. Fits owner-operators and small crews.
- **Workiz**: built around the phone call to dispatch chain, call recording, and local service ad integrations. Fits emergency-call-driven shops.

## Match the software to your job mix

**Garage door work splits into three job types with different software demands. Spring replacements are emergency dispatches that need truck stock visibility. Opener installs are scheduled sales jobs that need quotes and follow-up. Panel and full-door replacements are multi-visit jobs with deposits, ordering lead times, and progress invoicing. Score software against all three, not just the emergency call.**

### Spring jobs: the dispatch and parts test

A broken torsion spring call comes in at 7:40 AM. The customer cannot get the car out. Your dispatcher needs to see which tech is closest, which truck has the right spring sizes, and whether the tech can quote the conversion from one spring to two on the spot. If the software cannot show parts assigned to each truck, the tech guesses, sells from memory, and the cost of the spring never lands on the invoice. That is unbilled margin on your highest-volume job.

This is the same problem septic operators face with fittings and effluent pumps, which is why the [operator's comparison of septic company software](/guides/what-software-do-septic-companies-use/) weighs truck stock tracking so heavily.

### Opener installs: the quote and follow-up test

Opener replacements are scheduled, not dispatched. The customer is comparing you against a big-box install price. You need a quote with good-better-best options (chain drive, belt drive, wall-mount jackshaft), a way for the customer to approve from their phone, and an automated follow-up if they go quiet. Shops that quote openers by text message lose track of open estimates within a week.

### Panel and door jobs: the deposit and ordering test

A two-car door replacement runs across weeks: measure visit, deposit, order, install. The software needs deposits on the estimate, a job status that survives the ordering gap, and a final invoice that nets the deposit correctly. If your platform treats every job as a single visit, these jobs fall into a spreadsheet, and that is where margin errors live.

## What to test before you buy

**Run every finalist through one full simulated job on a phone, not a demo laptop: book a spring call, dispatch it, quote a two-spring conversion from the pricebook, attach four photos, swap a part off truck stock, take a card payment, and send the invoice. If any step needs the office, a workaround, or a second app, that platform will leak revenue on your busiest days.**

Use this field checklist during the trial period:

- [ ] Can the dispatcher see each tech's location and day capacity in one view?
- [ ] Can a tech see what springs, cables, rollers, and opener models are on their specific truck?
- [ ] Does the pricebook show flat-rate spring, opener, and panel pricing on mobile without a PDF hunt?
- [ ] Are photos required before a job can be marked complete?
- [ ] Can the tech add a part to the invoice from the truck, and does it decrement stock?
- [ ] Does the estimate follow-up sequence run without manual reminders?
- [ ] Does the invoice sync to QuickBooks or your accounting tool without double entry?
- [ ] Can a customer approve an opener quote and pay a deposit from their phone?
- [ ] Does the after-hours call flow create a dispatchable job, not just a message?
- [ ] Can you pull a customer's full door and opener history in under a minute for a callback?

Run the same test through the lens of the tech holding the phone in a driveway. The view from the truck is where adoption succeeds or fails, which is the framing behind the [technician dispatch software guide](/guides/technician-dispatch-software/) and applies directly here.

## When a full platform is the wrong buy

**A full field service platform is the wrong buy when a one-truck owner books under fifteen jobs a week, quotes every job personally, and has no dispatcher. In that case the per-technician fees and setup burden exceed the recovered margin. A leaner stack of scheduling, invoicing, and QuickBooks covers the record until a second truck or an office hire changes the math.**

The honest limitation: software does not fix a parts problem by itself. If springs are not counted onto trucks on Monday and reconciled on Friday, the best inventory module in the category will drift into fiction within a month. The platform enforces whatever discipline you bring to it. Shops that skip the weekly truck count find the same shrinkage they had on paper, now with a subscription fee attached.

Also note the integration ceiling. No mainstream option natively manages supplier ordering lead times for custom doors well. Most shops keep door ordering in a shared spreadsheet or a simple pipeline stage, and that is a reasonable workaround rather than a failure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best software for a small garage door company?

For one to three trucks, Housecall Pro, Jobber, and Workiz are the usual finalists because they combine scheduling, quoting, photos, and payments without enterprise setup. Choose Workiz if most work arrives as urgent phone calls, Jobber if you want the cleanest quoting flow, and Housecall Pro for the strongest all-around mobile app.

### Do garage door companies need inventory tracking in their software?

Yes, at the truck level. Springs, cables, drums, rollers, and opener models are sold off the truck on the first visit, so the software should assign stock to each vehicle and decrement it when a part hits the invoice. Without that, parts go unbilled and reorder quantities become guesswork.

### How do garage door companies handle after-hours emergency calls?

Most route after-hours calls to an answering service, an on-call tech's cell, or a call-tracking tool inside platforms like Workiz. The key requirement is that the call becomes a dispatchable job with a customer record attached, not a voicemail. Software plus a clear on-call rotation keeps response times under an hour for true emergencies like doors stuck open.

### Can garage door software create good-better-best opener quotes?

Yes. ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, and Jobber all support option-based estimates, so a tech can present chain, belt, and jackshaft opener packages side by side with photos and let the customer approve from a phone. This single feature meaningfully changes close rates on quoted install work.

### What should garage door software integrate with?

At minimum: QuickBooks or your accounting platform, a payment processor for on-site card collection, and your phone or call-tracking system. Larger shops add supplier catalogs and marketing attribution so spring-call ad spend ties back to booked revenue.
